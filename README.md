# <p align="center"> Aerosol and chemical compounds in the Atmosphere over Mérida, Yucatán State, Mexico </p>

Mérida is a municipality in the state of Yucatán and is the largest city in southeastern Mexico. Urban activities within the city create anthropogenic sources of pollutants and particulate matter that are ejected and suspended into the atmosphere. In addition to this, known atmospheric phenomena such as Saharan dust travels across the Caribbean and increases suspended particulate matter in the air, increasing dust nuisance. PM2.5 are fine suspended particles in the atmosphere with an aerodynamic diameter of less than 2.5µm. This particulate matter occurs with atmospheric gases such as carbon monoxide, nitrogen dioxide and sulphur dioxide which promote the production of ozone and exacerbate the greenhouse effect. This study uses cloud computation to extract open source air quality data, providing a time series geostatistical analysis of changes in atmospheric gases and aerosols over Mérida, Mexico. Satellite imagery from Sentinel-5P is used to validate the results using remote sensing techniques. The results show correlations between ozone and PM2.5 and sulphur dioxides which may suggest the influence of both urban activity as well as atmospheric sources on the hourly, monthly and yearly timescales. The geostatistical relationships between the atmospheric compounds investigated may therefore inform urban planning, public health measures and environmental engineering solutions for air quality compliance.

Keywords: Sentinel-5P, remote sensing, atmospheric science, environmental engineering, Mexico.
